What is copyright and why is it important?

Copyright is a legal right granted by the law of a country to the creators of original works of authorship including literary, dramatic, musical, artistic, and certain other intellectual works. This right helps to protect the creator’s investment in their work by preventing unauthorized reproduction or distribution. It’s crucial for maintaining control over how your works are used and ensuring you receive appropriate recognition and compensation.

What types of works can be protected by copyright?

Copyright protection can be applied to a wide range of creative works including books, music, paintings, photographs, films, software, and architectural designs, among others. Essentially, any original work fixed in a tangible medium of expression from which it can be perceived, reproduced, or otherwise communicated may be protected.

How long does copyright protection last?

In the United States, copyright protection typically lasts for the life of the author plus an additional 70 years after their death. For works made for hire, anonymous, or pseudonymous works, the protection lasts for 95 years from publication or 120 years from creation, whichever is shorter.

What is the process for filing a copyright?

The process involves submitting a completed application form, a non-refundable filing fee, and a non-returnable copy or copies of the work to be registered to the Copyright Office.

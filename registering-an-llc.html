Benefits of forming an LLC

When deciding which entity choice is most appropriate for a business in Virginia, the first and most important consideration is whether the structure protects the owner’s personal assets from liability.

This issue is especially important when the nature of a company’s business exposes it to a higher risk of lawsuits. In the event of a lawsuit where damages must be paid, compensation is paid using the company’s assets. However, company assets are often insufficient to cover all of the liabilities. In this case, a plaintiff could potentially come after the business owner’s personal assets to satisfy a judgment.

1

Limited liability

The great advantage of an LLC is explained in the name: the liability is limited. In other words, the plaintiff’s recovery is limited to the assets of the company. For a Virginia business owner, this makes forming an LLC a particularly advantageous entity choice. Unlike sole proprietorships or general partnerships, an LLC protects the owner’s property—such as their home, car, or bank accounts—from being targeted by those seeking damages against the company.

2

Tax efficiency

A second benefit of LLCs is the tax efficiency. Specifically, the LLC structure allows for pass-through tax treatment. Corporations are subject to what is known as double taxation. Double taxation occurs when company income is taxed first at the business level and then taxed a second time at the personal level. LLCs use pass-through taxation to avoid this problem by passing income directly to the owners’ personal tax returns. This method can potentially reduce overall tax rates. For many small business owners, the LLC structure is often paired with a tax filing as an “S-corporation,” which often leads to the most tax-efficient structure.

3

Simple, flexible management documents

A third major benefit to the LLC format is the simplicity and flexibility of the management documentation. Where a corporation often has articles, bylaws, and other various shareholder agreements, the LLC simply files articles of organization and then mainly relies on an operating agreement.

Documentation required to start an LLC

For business owners in Virginia forming an LLC, it is important to take care of all necessary paperwork and filing requirements. The business owner must select a company name that is available, file the articles of organization with the state, and pay the Virginia filing fee. The company will also have to obtain relevant licenses and permits applicable to the specific type of business it plans to operate.

Another important step in registering an LLC is drafting an agreement between the owners, usually referred to as an operating agreement. This document serves as the foundational governing document for how the company will operate and clearly defines the structure when a company has multiple owners.

The operating agreement sets forth the key issues about how the company will run its operations, such as voting power for each owner over important business decisions. In a single-member LLC, we at Avodah still recommend having a single-member operating agreement for banking and tax purposes, so we include it in our quick and easy LLC organization package.

If you have business partners in your LLC, a well-drafted operating agreement is not just recommended, it is essential.

The right operating agreement can help avoid extremely costly disputes between owners down the line and provide for smooth transitions in later events such as sales, transfers of interests, or estate changes.

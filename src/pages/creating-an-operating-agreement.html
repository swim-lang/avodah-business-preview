Creating an Operating Agreement in Virginia

A limited liability company (LLC) is a great choice for your business because it combines the best aspects of corporations and partnerships. LLC owners—known as members—enjoy pass-through profits to avoid the double taxation of corporations and are also afforded more protection from company liabilities than general partnerships.

LLCs are also popular because they avoid the extensive documentation, the reporting requirements, and the annual share-based registration fees of corporations. While you do not need to file an operating agreement with the state, we strongly recommend adopting one. Creating an operating agreement in Virginia is your guarantee that the company will be governed the way the owners determine in negotiations.

How LLCs work

LLC members can be individuals, corporations, other LLCs, or trusts. Members often contribute assets (called capital contributions) to launch the company. Capital contributions can be in cash, real estate, intellectual property, or expertise, which determines the members’ opening ownership interest.

What information does an LLC operating agreement contain?

A well-drafted LLC operating agreement is specific and detailed because it is the roadmap for managing, growing, and selling the company. When members reach stalemates and infighting begins, the agreement should have the answers to resolve the problems. Some of the basics your operating agreement should include are:

The basics
  • Transfer restrictions
  • Estate planning provisions
  • Access to records provisions
  • Tax and profit distribution policies
  • Names and ownership percentages of Members
  • Capital contribution and member cap table records
Daily operations
  • Dispute resolution
  • Accounting methods
  • Management provisions
  • Banking signatory powers
  • Voting on key business decisions
The agreement should also define
  • Amendment procedures
  • Enforcement provisions
  • Non-Compete provisions
  • Sale of the company processes
  • Liquidation or wind-down processes
  • Ownership of IP and confidentiality provisions
Members leaving LLCs

Oftentimes, unexpected disputes arise when members want to sell, retire, breach their fiduciary duty, or get a divorce. A good LLC operating agreement in Virginia will provide buy-out and transfer provisions that help the company smoothly navigate unexpected conflicts. Other times, it is new investment or sale opportunities that create uncertainty around buyouts or buy-ins. Whether in the good times or the tough times, a good operating agreement provides clarity and stability.
